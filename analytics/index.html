What this means

The numbers above in plain English: what is working, what is not, and why.

The good news first
When people find you, they act. 144 out of 594, about 24.2%, did something: called, asked for directions, or clicked through. That is a healthy rate. Nothing is wrong with how your listing presents you.
The problem is how few people see it
594 views in 6 months is a small number for a plumber on the Central Coast. This is not a persuasion problem, it is a visibility problem, and that is a different fix from the one we had been planning.
The phone is only ringing twice a month
13 calls in 6 months came through Google. We had wondered whether calls were happening that our website tracking simply could not see. Now we know: they were not. Google is contributing very little of your work at the moment.
Most of your listing activity can’t become a job
107 of your 144 interactions, 74.3%, were people asking Google for driving directions. You are a mobile plumber. Nobody drives to you. That usually means the listing is set up as a shopfront with an address showing, instead of as a business that travels to customers. It is free to change, and it is the first thing we are fixing.
You are barely showing up for the searches that matter
Google showed your listing for under 50 different search terms in 6 months, and three of the top five were people typing your own business name. Your website appears for 151 different searches. Your listing should be the one with the wider reach, not the narrower one. That gap is where new customers are being lost.
And it is happening in ordinary Google, not just Maps
65% of the people who saw your listing were in normal Google search results; only 35% were in Google Maps. We had assumed the map box was where everything was won and lost. The data says it is more even than that, so we are widening what we work on rather than betting on one place.

What we are doing about it

The moveWho
Fix how the listing is set up
Hide the street address, declare the suburbs you actually travel to, and check the business categories are right. This is the direction-requests problem, and it is free.
Us · this week
Widen what you show up for
Right now your listing appears for under 50 searches. Correct categories, service areas and a fuller profile is how that number grows.
Us · this month
Keep the reviews coming
Google says plainly that more reviews and better ratings help local ranking. You are on 6 at a perfect 5.0. The one-tap link is built and ready. It needs Beau asking on the job, and Emma’s past-customer list to fire the first wave.
You + us
Then, and only then, consider ads
There is little sense paying Google to send people to a listing that is not set up right. Once the free work is done we will look at a small, capped test. Google is currently offering you a A$600 new-advertiser credit, and we will read the terms on that first.

What we cannot see yet

Being straight with you about the gaps, so no number here is mistaken for the whole picture. These are on our list.

  • Enquiries through the website. The contact form is not yet wired to report submissions.
  • Click-to-call on the website. Taps on your phone number are not currently counted.
  • Which jobs came from where. Nothing joins a Google call to a quote or an invoice yet.
  • Cost per call and cost per job. These only exist once ads are running.
