Why it's staged and not one big deposit. NSW law caps the deposit on residential building work at 10% of the contract price, however large the job. What it does not cap is progress payments — so the money arrives as the work is done rather than all at the start. Each stage below has to be tied to work genuinely carried out, not to a date or to time on the job.
How payment worksterms and method
Each stage is invoiced when it is reached, with photographs of the completed work attached. Work on the following stage begins once the previous claim is paid. There is no interest, and no fee, for paying this way — the price is the same as it would be paid in one amount.
Because this job is over $20,000two extra legal requirements
Home Building Compensation cover must be in place first. For residential work over $20,000, cover must be taken out and the certificate given to the owner before any work starts and before any money is taken — including the deposit.

Cooling-off: the owner has 5 clear business days to withdraw after signing.

Scope of this document
This schedule sets out how and when the contract price is paid. It forms part of, and is read with, the written contract for the work — it is not the contract on its own. Residential building work in NSW valued over $5,000 including GST requires a written contract; work over $20,000 requires a large jobs contract and, where the Home Building Compensation Scheme applies, cover taken out and the certificate given to the owner before any work starts or any money is taken. Deposits on residential building work are capped at 10% of the contract price. Progress payments must relate to work actually carried out. Nothing in this schedule limits the consumer guarantees under the Australian Consumer Law or the statutory warranties under the Home Building Act 1989 (NSW), which cannot be excluded.
